description: Discipline for anything a person reads — replies, status updates, documents, pages, PR bodies, and messages drafted for someone else to send. Use before writing or sending text for a reader. The reader's next decision sets the content, order and words; the writer's process does not.

# reader-first

Text exists to change what its reader does next. Each rule below names a way writing fails its
reader: the answer buried, a word they cannot decode, a caveat they discover later, an item they
were waiting on lost inside a status.

## Order

- **Lead with what changes the reader's action.** Verdict first, then its receipt, then detail.
  A result that changes the plan or the understanding gets its own message or lead section, in
  full; routine status comes after it or separately. When the reader returns after being away,
  the first reply restates what changed while they were gone, whatever they asked. *(Receipt: a
  root cause explained at 2 AM sat under a timing update at 5 AM; the reader had to ask for it.)*
- **Deliver what someone is waiting on at once, as its own message.** A requested item (a link,
  a list of ids, a paste-ready line) is the first content of the reply; other work waits. Text
  between tool calls is not reliably seen. *(Receipt: three job ids written mid-turn; the reader
  was still waiting 30 minutes later.)*
- **In a document, each section opens with its claim and receipt; detail goes below.** Set up a
  comparison so the conclusion follows with no effort, then state it in one sentence. One figure
  per load-bearing claim; a table that carries a claim gets reading instructions. Lead
  comparisons with what the reader can see (side-by-side examples), tables after.

## Words

- **Use the reader's vocabulary.** Replace internal labels (run letters, codenames, section
  numbers of a private document, log tokens) with what they stand for, and give every metric a
  one-line legend at first use. Use the field's established name for a technique, with a checked
  reference; coin a term only when none exists, and say so. A word shown to mislead stays
  retired, even for the corrected quantity. *(Receipt: "clean 7/14" reused after "clean" was
  shown to count episodes that merely did not crash; the reader had to ask.)*
- **Say what happens, not the design's own vocabulary.** "The solver raises an error rather than
  ignore a constraint it cannot honor", not "it refuses loudly at the seam".
- **Times and dates go in the reader's clock**, read from the system, never inferred from a log
  stamp. *(Receipt: a UTC log stamp read as local time erased four working hours from a plan.)*

## Length and scope

- **Short by default: receipts stay, the prose around them goes.** Answer the question asked;
  skip process narration that changes no conclusion. Depth lives in the canonical record
  (pin-and-trace), not in the reply.
- **State what is not claimed, beside the claim.** Shortening must not overclaim; the
  qualifications are what survive the cut (ground-truth-discipline: conditions go where the
  claim is most prominent).
- **For readers outside the work, show only what they need to act.** Intermediate variants,
  internal process and provenance counters go to the internal record.
- **Text sent under someone else's name states only their facts and needs, in their voice and
  in the destination's format.** A paste target that cannot render tables gets plain bullets in
  a code block.

## Tone

- **Contribution, not competition.** Comparisons are matched-protocol measurements against a
  named reference, factual and cold, never "beats" or "wins". Ruthless with ideas, never with
  people.
